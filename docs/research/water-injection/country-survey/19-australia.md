# Australia — water in the combustion cycle (Hermes draft)
Country: Australia
Prompt language: English
Author: Hermes (box hjob)

## 1. Executive summary
Australia has documented work on water in the combustion cycle spanning from historical experiments by Yull Brown (1970s–1980s) through modern university research, CSIRO fuel-slurry engines, mining-industry hydrogen injection systems, and aftermarket water-methanol kits. Most documented cases focus on NOx and smoke emission reduction, with limited evidence of significant power or efficiency gains. No production road vehicles with water-injection as a primary system were found; documented systems are either laboratory prototypes, mining/industrial retrofits, aftermarket performance kits, or historical experiments.

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles
No production road vehicles with water-injection as a primary system were documented. Aftermarket water-methanol injection kits are widely available in Australia (Snow Performance, AEM, Aquamist) but are not OEM systems.

## 3. Research, prototypes, mining, and supplier systems

### 3.1 CSIRO DICE / bioDICE (water-slurry diesel)
- **Organization**: CSIRO (Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation), Australia
- **Years**: 2010s–present (ongoing)
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Modified diesel generator engines (large adapted diesel engines)
- **What was done**: Modified diesel engines to run on coal or biomass converted into a water-based slurry called micronised refined carbon (MRC). The fuel is directly injected into the adapted diesel engine.
- **Measured results**: No detailed performance numbers in open sources; program expenditure estimated at $61.8 million with projected benefit-cost ratio up to 8.6:1. Claims include increased electricity generation efficiency, significant greenhouse gas reduction, and reduced CO2 intensity compared to conventional coal technologies. [Source: csiro.au, research.csiro.au]

### 3.2 HYDI Hydrogen on Demand (mining, road trains, public transport)
- **Organization**: HYDI (Hydrogen Direct Injection), South Australian hydrogen technology company
- **Years**: 2010s–present (field trials and deployment)
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Large diesel engines in mining, heavy transport, and public transport
- **What was done**: Hydrogen produced on-demand from distilled water via proton exchange membrane electrolysis is injected into the air intake of diesel engines as a fuel supplement. Systems include HY2500 units for road trains and retrofits for buses and construction equipment.
- **Measured results**:
  - Holcim Pilbara road trains: up to 14% diesel fuel savings, 80% reduction in diesel particulate matter (DPM), reduced CO and NOx.
  - METRONET Byford Rail Extension CAT 962 loader: fuel consumption reduced from 8.18 L/h to 6.56 L/h (19% improvement) over 130 hours, using only 3.5 L distilled water.
  - UniSA 100 kVA diesel generator: diesel consumption reduced by 5–13%, DPM reduced by 25–80%, CO reduced by 7–25%.
  - Independent testing: reductions of up to 80% in DPM and 60% soot in engine oil.
- **Source links**: hydi.com.au, australianmining.com.au, metronet.wa.gov.au, iscouncil.org

### 3.3 University of Southern Queensland (USQ) – HHO and water injection in diesel generator
- **Organization**: University of Southern Queensland, Faculty of Engineering and Surveying
- **Years**: 2012
- **Engine/Vehicle**: 28 kW diesel generator (39 kW diesel engine coupled to 28 kW generator)
- **What was done**: Experimental validation of on-board HHO (Brown's Gas) addition with and without water injection into the air intake manifold. HHO produced by electrolysis, with water injection used to mitigate NOx increases.
- **Measured results** (from USQ project repository):
  - HHO increased diesel consumption proportionally to injection rate – up to 5.2% increase at 55% load with 6 L/min HHO.
  - NOx emissions reduced up to 11.8% with combined HHO and water injection.
  - Coefficient of performance of HHO compared to diesel: 1.19–1.85; required COP of 6.33–8.77 to break even with thermal losses.
- **Source**: University of Southern Queensland project repository (Rick Cameron 2012)

### 3.4 UNSW Sydney – Hydrogen-diesel direct injection dual-fuel
- **Organization**: UNSW Sydney, School of Mechanical and Manufacturing Engineering
- **Years**: 2022–2024
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Turbocharged Cummins ISBe 220 six-cylinder diesel engine
- **What was done**: Dual-fuel system maintaining original diesel injection and adding hydrogen direct injection into the cylinder. Water injection investigated to mitigate NOx increases from high hydrogen share.
- **Measured results** (Sharkey & Zare, Energies 2024):
  - Hydrogen energy ratio 90%: CO reduced 94%, CO2 reduced 96%, NOx increased ~70% (NO + NO2), NO increased 90%, NO2 increased 57%.
  - Water injection (700 mg/cycle) further reduced CO by ~40%, NO by up to 16%, NO2 by up to 83%.
  - Thermal and mechanical efficiencies improved; power increased ~8.8% with hydrogen, ~0.5% with water injection; BSFC improved.
  - Net NOx in many combined cases decreased compared to baseline diesel.
- **Source**: Energies, 2024; UNSW newsroom

### 3.5 University of Queensland (UQ) – Hydrogen port vs direct injection
- **Organization**: University of Queensland
- **Years**: 2020
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Single-cylinder diesel engine (derived from Euro 6d four-cylinder passenger car diesel)
- **What was done**: Comparison of hydrogen port injection and direct injection (DI) in a hydrogen-diesel dual-fuel engine. Water injection not a primary focus.
- **Measured results**: No specific water-injection numbers in open abstract; focused on knock and NOx differences between port and DI hydrogen injection.
- **Source**: UQ eSpace, AFMC2020 Revised Paper

### 3.6 Deakin University – Water injection in hydrogen/diesel dual-fuel
- **Organization**: Deakin University, School of Engineering
- **Years**: 2024
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Same Cummins ISBe 220 six-cylinder diesel as UNSW study
- **What was done**: Modelling and experiment on hydrogen-diesel dual-fuel with water injection to mitigate NOx increases.
- **Measured results**: Same key figures as UNSW study (CO, CO2, NOx, NO, NO2, power, efficiency, BSFC). Water injection reduced NOx increases from hydrogen.
- **Source**: Energies, 2024 (Sharkey & Zare)

### 3.7 Cool Runnings Engineering – Aquamist HFS3 water injection
- **Organization**: Cool Runnings Engineering, Australian performance engineering
- **Years**: 2020s (customer installation documented)
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Toyota Aristo (2JZ engine)
- **What was done**: Custom Aquamist HFS3 water injection system (50/50 water-methanol) integrated with Haltech 2500 ECU.
- **Measured results**: Customer-reported power surge to 449 kW with 29 psi boost when water injection activated.
- **Source**: coolrunningsengineering.com.au

## 5. Historical / racing / aviation-marine transfer (labeled)

### 5.1 Yull Brown – Brown's Gas (HHO) experiments (1970s–1980s)
- **Organization**: Yull Brown (Bulgarian-born Australian inventor), Auburn, Sydney
- **Years**: 1970s–1980s
- **Engine/Vehicle**: Mazda and Holden car engines
- **What was done**: Developed water electrolysis process to produce Brown's Gas (HHO) – a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen in ~2:1 ratio. Claimed to run vehicles on water-derived hydrogen. Patented in Australia (1977) and over 30 countries.
- **Measured results**: No verified performance or emissions data in open sources; claims include 1,000 miles per gallon of water and significant emissions reductions. Australian experts reportedly confirmed breakthrough in oxyhydrogen experiments.
- **Source**: Eagle-Research, waterjournal.org.au, Australian Post 1978, reptiliandimension.wordpress.com
- **Uncertainty**: Marketing claims and secondary sources only; no independent measurements in accessible literature.

### 5.2 HYDI – Hydrogen on Demand for mining and transport (2020s)

## Confirmed (spot-checked)

- **No Australian OEM series-production passenger car or commercial vehicle** with factory combustion-cycle water injection was found (Holden, Ford Australia, historic Chrysler/Mitsubishi Australia: nothing confirmed in this pass). Aftermarket water-methanol kits (Snow, AEM, Aquamist) are imported retail, not OEM or university maps. Do not use the Cool Runnings Engineering “449 kW / 29 psi” customer claim.

- **Sharkey and Zare, Deakin University, 2024,** *The Impact of Water Injection and Hydrogen Fuel on Performance and Emissions in a Hydrogen/Diesel Dual-Fuel Engine*, *Energies* 17(23) 5838, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/en17235838 . **Open MDPI PDF.** Authors are at Deakin (Waurn Ponds); the paper thanks QUT’s Biofuel Engine Research Facility and Richard Brown for supporting the **baseline** experiments. This is an **AVL Boost modelling study** of a turbocharged Cummins ISBe 220 31 (5.9 L) at 2000 r/min full load, with hydrogen energy ratios 0–90 % and intake-manifold water 0–700 mg/cycle. The experiment validated the **neat-diesel** model (in-cylinder pressure within about ±5 % near compression/ignition; power/torque within about ±0.60 %). It did **not** measure the hydrogen + water maps. Usable **simulated** water-injection increments at 700 mg/cycle: power about **+0.5 %**, IMEP about **+0.39–0.46 %**, thermal efficiency about **+0.5 %**, BSFC about **+0.5 %**, CO about **−40 %**, CO2 almost unchanged, NO up to **−16 %**, NO2 up to about **−83 %**, and total NOx down about **60–69 %** versus the same hydrogen fraction without water. The large CO/CO2 cuts (**94 % / 96 %**) and the **~8.8 %** power rise at 90 % hydrogen are **hydrogen-substitution** effects, not water-injection gains. 700 mg/cycle is a very large water rate (the paper’s Table 6: about **41.9 L/h**). Laboratory/model, not production.

- **Mortimer, Poursadegh, Brear, Yoannidis, Lacey, and Yang, University of Melbourne,** *Extending the knock limits of hydrogen DI ICE using water injection*, *Fuel* 335 (2023) 126652, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fuel.2022.126652 (PDF typically paywalled). ## Errors and unverified claims in the Hermes draft

- **Sharkey and Zare 2024 treated as a measured UNSW/Deakin engine map.** The authors are Deakin; the hydrogen + water results are **simulated**. Do not use 94/96/70/40/16/83 % as dynamometer measurements. The 8.8 % power figure is a hydrogen effect. Water’s simulated BSFC change is a slight **increase** (~0.5 %), not a BSFC improvement.

- **HYDI “hydrogen on demand,” Holcim 14 % / 80 % DPM, METRONET 19 % fuel cut, UniSA generator figures.** These systems electrolyse distilled water and inject **hydrogen** into the intake. Out of scope for this survey (water is a feedstock, not a combustion-cycle injectant). Company and news claims were not used as water-injection data.

- **Yull Brown / Brown’s Gas / HHO, “1,000 miles per gallon of water.”** Historical inventor claims, not independently measured combustion-cycle water injection. **Do not use.** Out of scope in the same way as HYDI.

- **USQ Rick Cameron 2012 (HHO with or without intake water): diesel consumption up 5.2 %, NOx down 11.8 %.** The repository page timed out in this pass (https://sear.unisq.edu.au/23108). **Do not use those percentages** until they are read from the opened open copy. Even then, separate HHO (out of scope) from any actual intake-water test.

- **“No documented Australian engine testing with water-diesel emulsions.”** False; see Ghojel/Honnery/Tran above.

- **“No Australian patents (AU) found.”** Not independently exhausted. Do not treat as a proof of absence.

- **Alsaadi et al., IOP 2018 emulsion-stability paper** and Lif/Nydén/Holmberg microemulsion work: the latter is primarily Chalmers/Swedish (later Macquarie affiliation for Nydén). Stability papers are not engine maps.

- **Cool Runnings / Aquamist 449 kW.** Customer/shop claim. **Do not use.**
