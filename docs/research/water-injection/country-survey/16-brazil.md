## 1. Executive summary

This research identified limited documented cases of water-injection systems in Brazilian automotive applications. ## Confirmed (spot-checked)

- **No Brazilian OEM series-production passenger car or commercial vehicle** with factory combustion-cycle water injection was found (Volkswagen do Brasil, Fiat/Stellantis Brasil, Mercedes-Benz do Brasil, Scania Brasil, Volvo Brasil, Petrobras, historic FNM/Gurgel/Puma: nothing confirmed in this pass). Bosch LTDA and UFMG/CTM work below is research or vehicle demonstration, not a factory system.

- **André Sperl, Unicamp / MWM International Motores, 2009 professional master's dissertation,** *Utilização de emulsões de água em óleo Diesel combustível para redução das emissões de poluentes em motores de combustão interna ciclo Diesel* (*Use of water in Diesel oil emulsions as a fuel to reduce pollutant emissions in Diesel internal combustion engines*), advisor Celso Aparecido Bertran. Open record: https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12733/1611365 and DOI https://doi.org/10.47749/t/unicamp.2009.477281 . Also summarized in the open Jornal da Unicamp (13–19 Sept 2010): https://www2.unicamp.br/unicamp/unicamp_hoje/ju/setembro2010/ju474_pag02.php . Six fuels, including three emulsions with the same **22 % water by mass** and different patented surfactants, tested on a **3.0 L** high-speed diesel with an unchanged Euro III heavy-duty calibration, 13-mode stationary cycle NBR 15634 (2008). Emulsion B versus conventional diesel: **NOx down 22 %**, **particulate matter down 42 %**, **specific fuel consumption up 8 % on average**. Bench test of about 30 days; not a production emulsion fuel and not a field fleet trial. (The newspaper headline mistakenly says “óxido de carbono”; the body and the thesis abstract say NOx.) **Missed by the Hermes draft.**

- **Dantas Neto, Fernandes, Barros Neto, Castro Dantas, and Moura, UFRN, 2011,** *Alternative fuels composed by blends of nonionic surfactant with diesel and water: engine performance and emissions*, *Brazilian Journal of Chemical Engineering* 28(3) 521–531, DOI https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-66322011000300017 . **Open SciELO PDF** (not paywalled): https://www.scielo.br/j/bjce/a/HFvLT9vyb3K9QmfJwWDqVnt/?format=pdf . Stationary diesel, water-in-oil microemulsions with **15 wt %** ethoxylated (5 EO) nonylphenol. Only systems with **up to 6 % water** met Brazilian diesel/biodiesel specifications. Specific fuel consumption of the microemulsions was **greater than neat diesel**. Versus diesel: **CO and NOx increased**, **black smoke decreased**. Within the microemulsions, NOx fell as water content rose; the increase versus diesel is attributed to the large surfactant fraction. The paper also cites Brazilian patent deposit **PI 0600616-7 A** (2006), diesel-based microemulsion fuel.

- **Lauro de Oliveira Júnior, Universidade de Brasília, 10 July 2013 undergraduate monografia,** *Análise dos efeitos da injeção de água na performance e característica de emissões em motores diesel de injeção direta utilizando misturas diesel-biodiesel*. **Open UnB PDF** (not “institutional access required”): https://bdm.unb.br/handle/10483/6027 and https://bdm.unb.br/bitstream/10483/6027/1/2013_LauroDeOliveiraJunior.pdf . Intake-manifold water mist on a small stationary diesel (shaft loads about 1, 6 and 11 kW) with B5/B20. The abstract’s “effective” water range is **0.15–1.15 kg/h**. The author reports an SFC reduction of **up to 10 %** at low and medium load for 10 % and 50 % water-to-fuel ratios, no reduction at higher load, and an SFC increase at a 100 % ratio. **NOx was only semi-quantitative** (no calibration-gas standard). Table 4.1’s “up to 17 %” SFC figure is **B20 versus B5, not a water-injection gain** — do not use it as a WI result. Undergraduate bench work; use cautiously.

- **Deborah Domingos da Rocha, UFMG, 2018 master's dissertation,** *Estudo da injeção de água e alteração da razão volumétrica de compressão para mitigação da detonação e melhoria do desempenho de um motor de combustão interna*, advisor Ramon Molina Valle. Open UFMG record and PDF: https://repositorio.ufmg.br/items/e007f419-e628-4ab7-aec8-6e32105ed60c . 3D CFD (STAR-CD) of an E27 engine, CR 15:1, 2000 r/min, 6 bar IMEP, first validated against a knocking experimental baseline, then used to study port and direct water injection at about 10–25 % water-to-fuel and a CR cut to 14:1. **Simulated** best case, 10 % direct water after retiming to the knock limit: indicated specific fuel consumption down about **3.50 %**, indicated power up **3.89 %**, IMEP up **0.84 %**. These percentages are numerical, not a measured water-injection map.

- **da Rocha, Radicchi, Lopes, Brunocilla, Gomes, Santos, Malaquias, Rodrigues Filho, and Baêta, 2021,** *Study of the water injection control parameters on combustion performance of a spark-ignition engine*, *Energy* 217, 119346, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2020.119346 . UFMG with Bosch-affiliated co-authors. Open abstract (PDF paywalled; no legitimate open PDF opened in this pass): experimental water-injection control study on a **single-cylinder research engine** at **1500 r/min**, **5 and 8 bar IMEP**, then a validated 3D model extrapolated to 10 bar IMEP and 3000 r/min. Water **temperature** did not significantly change fuel-conversion efficiency. Proper injection **timing** and **pressure** reduced ISFC by **3 %** and **3.5 %** respectively, with a larger effect at the higher load. **Missed as a numbered result by the draft.**

- **Thiago Rodrigo Vieira da Silva, Netto, Santos, Malaquias, and Baêta, 2022,** *Development Procedure for Performance Estimation and Main Dimensions Calculation of a Highly-Boosted Ethanol Engine with Water Injection*, *Energies* 15(13) 4656, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/en15134656 . **Open MDPI PDF** (not paywalled). UFMG CTM, with one AVL Graz co-author. Design / **1D GT-Power prediction** of a new 1.0 L, 3-cylinder, ethanol-only (E100), CR 14:1, direct-injection, port-water-injection prototype. Predicted: **279 N·m** from 2000–4000 r/min, **135 kW** indicated at 5500 r/min, maximum **WFR 19.2 %**, maximum indicated fuel-conversion efficiency **45.3 %** at 3000 r/min, and over **40 %** on almost the entire full-load curve. The same figures appear in the open UFMG doctoral thesis (2022): https://repositorio.ufmg.br/handle/1843/50746 . **Do not treat 45.3 % / 279 N·m / 135 kW as measured bench or vehicle results.** The paper cites Baêta et al., *Applied Thermal Engineering* 139 (2018) 35–46, DOI 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2018.04.112, as experimental reference data (including a comparison point with WFR 10.7 % at 2500 r/min and about 33 bar IMEP). That 2018 PDF was not opened in this pass (paywalled; no legitimate open copy found).

- **Lopes, Brunocilla, Franco, Oliveira Junior, Lopes, and Gomes, Robert Bosch LTDA, SAE 2021-36-0082,** *Water injection enabling high efficiency flex fuel engines development*, SAE Brasil 2021, DOI https://doi.org/10.4271/2021-36-0082 . Real paper; **open official abstract** (PDF typically paywalled). Water injection used so that geometric compression ratio can be set for ethanol, with knock control. Vehicle energy efficiency in MJ/km improved **up to 7.3 %** (1.59 → 1.47 MJ/km) on a naturally aspirated port-injection vehicle and **up to 4.8 %** (1.85 → 1.76 MJ/km) on a turbocharged direct-injection vehicle. These are **vehicle energy-efficiency figures from a higher CR enabled by water injection**, not a same-compression-ratio water-only BSFC map.

- **Jair Leopoldo Loaiza Bernal, Unicamp, 2019 doctoral thesis,** *Study of the operation of an internal combustion engine, fueled by indirect injection of ethanol and direct injection of water into the combustion chamber* (Portuguese title in the record), advisor Janito Vaqueiro Ferreira; FAPESP 15/20801-3 with a Peugeot Citroën cooperation note. Related paper: Loaiza Bernal and Ferreira, *Model of water injection process during closed phase of spark ignition engine*, *Energy* 174 (2019) 1121–1132, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2019.03.037 (PDF paywalled). Open abstracts describe a two-zone thermodynamic model of direct water injection, compared with published gasoline water-injection experiments (maximum BSFC error about 11 %). **Not a measured Brazilian engine map.** The Unicamp bitstream URL tried in this pass returned 404; the bibliographic record remains. **Missed by the Hermes draft.**

- **Unicamp “hydrogel for water removal from diesel.”** Correctly out of scope (fuel drying, not water in the combustion cycle), but the draft missed Sperl 2009 and Loaiza Bernal 2019 at the same university.

- **Autoentusiastas historical article:** not used as measured data.

- Do not use UnB Table 4.1’s 17 % SFC figure as a water-injection result. Do not use the Jornal da Unicamp “óxido de carbono 22 %” headline. Do not use the UFMG 45.3 % / 279 N·m / 135 kW figures, or the 2018 Rocha 3.50 / 3.89 / 0.84 % figures, as if they were measured production or dynamometer water-injection maps.
