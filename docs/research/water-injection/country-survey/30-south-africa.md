## 1. Executive summary
South Africa has a modest but documented history of water-in-combustion research concentrated in three domains: (1) diesel–water emulsion work at the University of the Witwatersrand in the 1980s–1990s (Crookes et al.), reporting measured reductions in specific fuel consumption, NOx, and soot with 10–15% water emulsions; (2) gas-turbine water-injection research at the University of Pretoria (HEFAT 2011, 2012) focused on compressor cooling and droplet evaporation at high pressure/temperature, not reciprocating engines; (3) passing mentions of water injection as a NOx-control option in CSIR and University of Pretoria mining-sector dual-fuel studies (platinum-mine load-haul-dump vehicles), but no published engine-test data. The Sasol Advanced Fuels Laboratory at UCT has extensive HCCI and combustion-imaging capability but no open publications on water injection for knock suppression. No South African production road vehicle has shipped with factory water injection. Aftermarket water–methanol kits exist but are imported (Aquamist UK, Snow Performance US); no local aftermarket manufacturer was identified. No six-stroke water-injection engine prototype or patent was found. Aviation/marine/mining transfer: the only documented link is the mention of EGR + water injection in a CSIR/UP mining dual-fuel study, but no combustion-cycle water injection hardware was tested on mining engines.
