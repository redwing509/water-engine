### Official Iranian maps that can be used (with the comparisons they actually state)

- Official Mohammad Reza Seifi and Seyed Reza Hassan-Beygi (College of Abouraihan, University of Tehran; Seifi corresponding, mrseifi@ut.ac.ir), Barat Ghobadian (Tarbiat Modares University, Tehran), and Umberto Desideri and Marco Antonelli (DESTEC, University of Pisa), “Experimental investigation of a diesel engine power, torque and noise emission using water–diesel emulsions,” *Fuel* 166 (2016) 392–399, DOI 10.1016/j.fuel.2015.10.122. Official University of Pisa ARPI institutional copy opened: https://arpi.unipi.it/retrieve/e0d6c930-af97-fcf8-e053-d805fe0aa794/final%20fuel%20black.pdf. Iranian-led engine work (University of Tehran and Tarbiat Modares Bioenergy Research Center acknowledged), with Pisa co-authors. Engine: Massey Ferguson MF 399 tractor, Perkins A63544 direct-injection diesel, official body maximum power 110 hp (82 kW) at 2300 rpm. Official tests: water–diesel emulsions at 2, 5, 8 and 10% water by volume; diesel purchased at a Tehran filling station; power and torque at 1400–1900 rpm; noise at 1600–1900 rpm and 25–100% load; no engine-hardware changes. Official abstract and official body: the 2% water emulsion significantly increased engine power (body: P < 0.01) without a significant increase in noise. The official body does not state a percentage for that power increase, and none is taken. Official abstract also says the 2% emulsion increased torque significantly; the official body says adding water up to 5% does not change torque significantly, and that raising water to 8% reduced torque by about 1.4% compared with the 5% blend (not compared with diesel). The official E2 torque-increase claim is therefore not taken as settled. Official conclusions bullet 1 (“no such effects were found for engine torque”) disagrees with the official abstract and body ANOVA statements that fuel type was significant for torque, and is not used. Official body: raising water to 10% by volume “caused the engine power to decrease by 2%”; the comparison baseline is not restated in that sentence and that 2% figure is not taken as a settled map versus diesel. This paper is a power, torque, and noise study; it is not a NOx or soot map.
