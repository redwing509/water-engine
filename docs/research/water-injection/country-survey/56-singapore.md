Water is not treated as a fuel in this note. Where a source discusses a methanol–water fuel composition or a water-in-diesel emulsion, water is a component of that mixture, not a fuel.

## Verification (checker)

**No Singapore production or near-production road vehicle** with factory water injection, water-methanol injection or water-fuel emulsion was found in this pass. Measured engine work that can be used is laboratory testing at the **National University of Singapore, Department of Mechanical Engineering**, on water-in-diesel emulsions (2012–2013). Other Singapore-linked items are an industrial gas-turbine steam-injection case, a California retrofit of a U.S.-flagged containership named *APL Singapore*, aftermarket methanol/water premix marketing, a Singapore publication of an Australian methanol–water patent family, and a student assignment that was not opened.

### Usable figures

- NUS ED10 emulsion (*Applied Energy* 2013, official abstract): 10% water-in-diesel, 2.5 L Toyota 2KD-FTV, 25–100% load, 800–3600 rpm; comparable cylinder pressure and heat release; slightly less power and efficiency, higher BSFC; lower exhaust temperature and NO at all loads; higher CO at light load and low speed.
- NUS nano-organic emulsion (*Applied Energy* 2013, official abstract): 82.4% diesel, 5% water, 12.6% nano-organic additives by volume; better brake thermal efficiency and lower NOx than diesel; slightly longer ignition delay and shorter combustion duration. No percentage magnitudes in the abstract.
- NUS nano-organic emulsion (*Fuel* 2013, EPA HERO abstract): when “10% emulsion fuel was used,” BTE +14.2% and NOx −30.6% vs pure diesel. This is not combined with the 5%-water recipe above.
- PCS GT10 steam injection (third-party case-study copy): NOx from 300–400 mg/MJ fuel to just below 100 mg/MJ; unit commissioned June 1997. Industrial gas turbine.
- *APL Singapore* WIFEOD (Professional Mariner / Santa Barbara APCD): expected 1% NOx reduction per 1% water, test goal up to 25% — **goals, not measured results**.
- Everything else: no verified numbers, or not Singapore engine research.

## Corrections to Hermes (summary)

1. The 14.2% BTE and 30.6% NOx figures belong to the *Fuel* 2013 paper (“10% emulsion fuel”), not to the *Applied Energy* 2013 paper that gives the 82.4/5/12.6 composition (5% water). Those two results were not merged.
2. The Springer DOI given for the Singapore Polytechnic assignment is a Japanese rocket-detonation chapter and was dropped. Assignment numbers were not used because the slides were not opened.
3. The *APL Singapore* ship is **United States flagged**, not Singapore-flagged; the retrofit is a California programme with a Canadian vendor. The 25% NOx figure is a test goal.
4. Two Diesel Emulsification URLs (`/marine/`, `/marine/nox-compliance/`) returned 404; sister-craft protocol details were not on the Singapore page that opened.
5. SG190380A1 is a Singapore publication of an Australian Gane Energy family; 40–50 °C and DME were not verified.
6. The 2023 ramjet paper is Chinese work; a NUS researcher only reviewed it. Dropped.
7. Funding, “stable >1 year,” and named surfactants were not confirmed in the abstracts opened.
8. “Same 2.5 L Toyota test bed” for all three NUS papers was not confirmed; only Fahd 2013 is explicitly the 2KD-FTV in the pages opened.
9. The simulator follow-up called the NUS solvers “open source (GPL, as part of OpenFOAM).” The official pages do not state GPL and do not offer a public source repository; that license wording was not kept.
10. The NSCC portfolio page does not name RYrhoCentralFoam.

## Simulators and modeling tools

Five NUS Laboratory for Energy Innovation (Huangwei Zhang group) solver pages were opened and match the named tools. None is a vehicle water-injection or water-diesel-emulsion engine-cycle model. No public source-repository URL was found; the pages do not state a license name (they are described as built on or compatible with OpenFOAM). They are candidates only; nothing was installed.

- **RYrhoCentralFoam** — NUS Computational Combustion and Energy Group. Custom OpenFOAM solver (based on rhoCentralFoam) for multi-phase compressible reacting flows, with a Cantera interface. Official page: https://blog.nus.edu.sg/huangwei/nus-numerical-solver/nus-ryrhocentralfoam-solver/ . The same page lists NUS papers on detonations and on water-spray / water-cloud interactions with detonations (for example Xu & Zhang, *Combust. Flame* 245 (2022) 112369). License name not stated; no public git/download link (contact listed for case files). Hermes’ claim that an NSCC 2024 case study used this named solver was not confirmed: that page only says “OpenFOAM based solvers.”
- **dropletInterCanteraFoam** — Shangpeng Li / Jiang Zhiwu (per the lab page). Droplet evaporation, ignition and combustion with VoF and Cantera. https://blog.nus.edu.sg/huangwei/dropletintercanterafoam/ . License and public repo not stated.
- **ASURF-2Phase** — NUS work by Qiang Li and Yijie Zhuang, built on the ASURF gaseous solver of Zheng Chen (Peking University). Eulerian–Eulerian two-phase reacting flows; spray flames and hydrogen detonation mitigation. https://blog.nus.edu.sg/huangwei/asurf-2phase/ . License and public repo not stated.
- **reactingPbeFoam** — Cao Chenyang (per the lab page). OpenFOAM soot/particle PBE–OpenQBMM solver, not a water-injection tool. https://blog.nus.edu.sg/huangwei/reactingpbefoam/ . License and public repo for this solver not stated.
- **BatteryFoam** — Ding Yan (per the lab page). Lithium-ion battery thermal-runaway / fire-hazard solver, not engine combustion. https://blog.nus.edu.sg/huangwei/nus-batteryfoam-solver/ . License and public repo not stated.

**Not added:** Cantera (already in the shared candidate/installed list; these solvers interface with it); OpenFOAM itself (upstream); DYMAIN (NTU 1997 aircraft gas-turbine dynamic simulation — thesis handle 10356/19978 was not opened, HTTP 405). No Singapore-developed GT-Power/WAVE-class tool or water-injection-specific engine-cycle code was found.

Verified tools were appended to the private candidate list. Installing any of them still needs a separate OK.
