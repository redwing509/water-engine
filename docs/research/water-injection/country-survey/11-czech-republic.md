## Executive Summary
This report surveys documented cases in the Czech Republic (including the former Czechoslovakia territory) where water was injected **into the combustion cycle** of road‑vehicle engines (spark‑ignition or diesel). The material spans patents, open‑access journal articles, conference papers and university theses from 1926 – 2026. Most work is recent (2015 – 2026) and focuses on **self‑contained water‑injection systems** for passenger‑car engines, often in partnership with foreign firms (e.g., Hanon Systems Germany, FEV Europe Germany). No production‑series road vehicle with factory‑fitted water injection has been commercialised in the Czech Republic to date.

## 8. Limits and Open Questions
* Quantitative performance data (power gain, BSFC reduction) for the Czech‑developed WAHASY demonstrator remain unpublished; only qualitative statements are available.
* The scalability of exhaust‑gas condensate harvesting to mass‑production vehicles is unproven.
* Integration of water‑injection with modern gasoline direct‑injection (GDI) engines in Czech‑manufactured cars has not been demonstrated.
* Potential regulatory impacts (e.g., EU RDE limits) on adoption of water‑injection in the Czech market need further study.

## Confirmed (spot-checked)

- **No Czech OEM series-production road vehicle** (Škoda, Tatra, LIAZ, or others) with factory combustion-cycle water injection was found.

- **Bazala, Hébert, Fischer, Nothbaum, Thewes, Voßhall, Diehl, and Kučera, 2021,** *Research and Development of Self-Contained Water Injection Systems*, *Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health* 18(10), 5392, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18105392 , open access (MDPI): https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/10/5392 . This is the WAHASY (WAter HArvesting SYstem) paper previously noted in the France check as PMID 34070124 (Hanon/FEV/Brno, not IFPEN). Czech affiliations: **Jiri Bazala** and **Guillaume Hébert**, Hanon Systems Autopal Services s.r.o., **Hluk, Czech Republic**; Bazala and **Pavel Kučera**, Institute of Automotive Engineering, **Brno University of Technology**. German partners: Hanon Systems Deutschland (Kerpen) and FEV Europe (Aachen). Funding: BUT FSI-S-20-6267. Label the partnership.
  - The paper is mainly a **methodology and vehicle demonstration of on-board exhaust-condensate harvesting**, not a new engine BSFC/NOx map. A vehicle prototype was built on **FEV’s Audi TT-S water-injection demonstrator**. At a constant-speed point chosen to match ~88 s of WLTC (3rd gear, 3,500 rpm), measured **WAHASY harvest efficiency was 90 %** (CFD droplet-separator estimate 95 %). That **90 % is water-recovery efficiency, not indicated thermal efficiency**.
  - The paper argues that typical cycle water demand is mostly under **10 % WFR**, so a harvest efficiency of about **8–15 %** would suffice, and that ~**300 mL** of harvested water would cover the first 1,000 s of WLTC in their estimate.
  - **Do not treat as Czech measured engine results:** (1) “40–50 % WFR with PWI has the same effect as 10 % EGR” is cited from **Conway 2019** (San Antonio), not measured on this demonstrator; (2) Figure 1 power/torque is from the FEV water-injection website, not a new Czech dynamometer map; (3) the closing “more than 3 % fuel consumption” remark is a literature/figure-based CO₂ illustration, not a TT-S test-map number from this paper.

- **US 2022/0003156 A1** (published 6 Jan 2022). Real application. Inventors Radek Malec, Martin Riegel, and Petr Novák, České Budějovice, Czech Republic. Rail/valve hardware for injecting water into a combustion chamber or intake tract, with a reduced-dead-volume insert. **No experimental map.**

- **Justin Jacob Thomas et al., *International Journal of Hydrogen Energy*, DOI 10.1016/j.ijhydene.2025.152822,** *Mitigation of abnormal combustion in hydrogen-fuelled SI engines via ignition system optimisation and water injection.* A bibliographic record exists and is associated with Czech Technical University in Prague. The draft’s claim that the article is open access was not confirmed here (Elsevier IJHE is typically paywalled). **Do not use “up to 87 % NOx reduction with a 2 % fuel-consumption increase”** until those figures are read from a legitimate open copy.

## Errors and unverified claims in the Hermes draft

- **40–50 % WFR = 10 % EGR, and qualitative “Fig. 1” power/torque, attributed to the Czech Audi TT-S test.** Those are cited or third-party figures in the WAHASY paper, not this team’s measured engine map.

- **“90 %” if read as engine efficiency:** it is condensate-harvest efficiency.

- **Thomas et al. 87 % NOx / 2 % FC, and “open-access”:** not taken from an opened open copy in this pass.

- **Toman et al. “≥40 kg/h” condensation, and a KOKA 2025 paper pointed at a 2021 PDF URL:** not verified from an opened source. The VUT dspace heat-exchanger record may exist as supporting hardware work; do not use a condensation-rate number until the file is opened.
