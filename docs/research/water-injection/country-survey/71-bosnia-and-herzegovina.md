### Sources opened

- **Borborean A T, Stoica V, Lelea D, "Test bench for studying the effects of water injection inside an internal combustion engine", IOP Conf. Ser.: Mater. Sci. Eng. 477 (2019) 012012**, https://doi.org/10.1088/1757-899X/477/1/012012 (open access, CC BY 3.0; full text opened). Authors are at Politehnica University of Timisoara (Romania). It appeared in the proceedings of the International Conference on Applied Sciences, 9–11 May 2018, Banja Luka, Bosnia and Herzegovina. **The work is Romanian; only the conference was in Bosnia.**
  - Setup: a 1.6 L Daewoo engine run in wasted-spark configuration; water injected upstream of the throttle by a single injector, twice per crankshaft rotation; timing and quantity controlled by a CompactRIO device with a LabView algorithm.
  - Result quoted in the paper: "a decrease in NOx emission values with an average of 30%" in a simple with/without-water run; it also notes that injection timing matters, not only quantity. It cites another study (Mingrui) at 34.6 % NOx reduction for 15 % water.
  - Paper's own conclusion: "water injection is a good field of study and without many published results."
- **Marmara University (Turkey) record of Yılmaz M., Gül M. Z., Köten H., Binark A. K., "Injection Parameters and Geometrical Effects on The Emissions of a Heavy Duty CI Engine", 11th International Combustion Symposium, Sarajevo, 24–27 June 2010**, https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/a1489cf1-9aa2-4df6-b7b7-5f3d6b1565d1/injection-parameters-and-geometrical-effects-on-the-emissions-of-a-heavy-duty-ci-engine (page opened, HTTP 200). The record gives title, authors and venue only: no abstract, no mention of water. **Turkish authors; Sarajevo is only the venue; nothing shows water injection.**
