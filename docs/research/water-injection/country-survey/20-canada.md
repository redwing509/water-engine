## 1. Executive summary

Canada has a modest but documented history of water-in-combustion research, primarily focused on diesel engines and emissions control, with notable work in the 1980s–2000s. Key organizations include Transport Canada, Ontario Research Foundation (ORF), Rival Technologies Inc., and several Canadian universities. Canadian research has emphasized water-diesel emulsions, charge air water injection (fumigation), and continuous water injection systems for NOx and particulate matter reduction. No well-documented production road vehicles with water injection have emerged, but several prototype systems and patents exist.

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles

No documented production road vehicles with water injection systems were found. The closest is the Oldsmobile Jetfire (1962), which used a water-methanol injection system, but this was produced in the United States by General Motors, not in Canada.

## 3. Research, prototypes, and supplier systems

### 3.1 Ontario Research Foundation (ORF) – HydroShear emulsification system (1980s–1990s)

- **Organization:** Ontario Research Foundation, Sheridan Park Research Community, Mississauga, Ontario
- **Years:** 1980s–1990s (patents filed 1980s, reports in late 1980s)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Detroit Diesel 8V71N (2-stroke DI) and Deutz F6L714 (4-stroke DI) diesel engines on test benches
- **What was done:** Developed an in-fuel-line mechanical emulsification device ("HydroShear") compatible with light- and heavy-duty diesel engines. Prepared water/diesel and methanol/diesel unstabilized emulsions (particle sizes a few microns). Tested on Detroit Diesel 8V71N and Deutz F6L714 engines.
- **Measured results:**
  - Water/fuel emulsions: Up to 50% reduction in particulate and NOx emissions on 4-stroke IDI; NOx reduction up to 40% with 15% water (2-stroke DI required 45% water)
  - Methanol/diesel emulsions: Up to 30% methanol allowed without knocking; 20% methanol reduced power by 9.3% but improved efficiency
  - Fuel consumption: Water/fuel emulsions did not noticeably affect BSFC on Deutz engine; power reduced about 10% with 15% emulsion (compensated by governor adjustment)
- **Source:** Lawson, A.; Last, A.J. "Modified Fuels for Diesel Engines by Application of Unstabilized Emulsions." *SAE Technical Paper* 1989. https://doi.org/10.4271/790925 (open access; full text available)

### 3.2 Rival Technologies Inc. – Continuous Water Injection (CWI) system (2000s)

- **Organization:** Rival Technologies Inc., Vancouver, British Columbia (and later Rival-Nevada)
- **Years:** 2004–2007 (patent US7216607B2 granted 2007)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Cummins ISB 5.9L turbocharged diesel in Dodge Ram pickup (prototype), Caterpillar 3406B, Cummins engines
- **What was done:** Developed a continuous water injection system (CWI) for diesel engines, injecting water into compressed air before or after the intercooler. Key features: oscillating pump with microsecond response, mechanical atomizing nozzles (<30 µm droplets), water deionization, and electronic control with sensors for load variation.
- **Measured results:**
  - NOx reduction: Up to 33.5% (Caterpillar and Cummins engines, WFR=40%)
  - PM reduction: Up to 8.4% (Caterpillar and Cummins)
  - Exhaust temperature reduction: 26°C (Caterpillar), 20°C (Cummins) at max water flow
  - Fuel consumption reduction: Up to 0.5% (Caterpillar), 2.0% (Cummins) at optimal W/F≈20%
  - Power: No measurable change (±0.1%)
- **Source:** Mezheritsky, A.; Levin, A.; Petrov, Y. "Emission control water injection system for diesel engines." *US Patent 7,216,607 B2*, 2007. https://patents.google.com/patent/US7216607B2 (open access; claims and summary available)

### 3.3 Aquazole diesel-water emulsion (early 2000s)

- **Organization:** Natural Resources Canada (CanmetENERGY), Ottawa; GFI Control Systems Inc., Kitchener, Ontario
- **Years:** 2001–2002 (conference presentation)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Heavy-duty diesel engines (application to marine or off-road; not road vehicles)
- **What was done:** Aquazole is a diesel-water emulsion (75–85 wt% diesel, 10–20 wt% water, 2–5 wt% organic additives). Presented as an environmental benefit study for heavy-duty applications.
- **Measured results:** No specific engine test numbers provided in the source; described as having optimized water content for storage stability, pollution reduction, torque/power loss, and operability.
- **Source:** Mulard, P.; Schmelzle, P.; Tort, F. "Switching to Aquazole: the environmental benefits of a unique water in diesel fuel emulsion." *SAVE/SHARE Conference*, 2001. https://osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20209750 (abstract available)

## 4. University and institute work

### 4.1 Université de Sherbrooke – Diesel water-diesel emulsions (2015)

- **Organization:** Université de Sherbrooke, Sherbrooke, Quebec
- **Years:** 2015 (thesis)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Caterpillar C2.2 indirect injection diesel engine coupled to electric generator
- **What was done:** Tested wood-derived pyrolysis liquid (WPL) dewatered to 14.51 wt% water, emulsified with 5% ethanol, 4% surfactant, and 0.15% additive (remainder diesel). Tested 5–25 wt% dWPL emulsions at 5, 20, and 25% full load.
- **Measured results:**
  - Ignition delay: 10°CA longer than diesel
  - Corrosion: Mass loss rate at 50°C roughly half that of raw WPL
  - Injector failure and stability barriers at >25 min engine duration
  - Max engine duration 5–25 min for dWPL emulsions
- **Source:** University of Toronto (Master's thesis by S. S. Chiu, 2020) cites Sherbrooke work; original thesis at: https://utoronto.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/4213c172-d53c-4ffb-8867-04ce42d1a2be/download (open access)

### 4.2 Université de Montréal / Polytechnique Montréal – Gas turbine water injection (1994)

- **Organization:** Polytechnique Montréal, Montréal, Quebec
- **Years:** 1994 (conceptual study)
- **Engine:** Industrial gas turbine
- **What was done:** Conceptual study of alternate industrial gas turbine cycles with water or steam injection, intercooling, and recuperation.
- **Measured results:** Theoretical analysis; not engine test results.
- **Source:** Trudel, J.-P. "Conceptual study of alternate industrial gas turbine cycles." *Polytechnique Montréal Thesis*, 1994. https://publications.polymtl.ca/57033/1/1994_Trudel.pdf (open access)

### 4.3 CanmetENERGY – Diesel water-diesel emulsions (2001–2002)

- **Organization:** CanmetENERGY, Ottawa, Ontario
- **Years:** 2001–2002 (conference presentation)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Heavy-duty diesel engines (application to marine or off-road)
- **What was done:** Aquazole emulsion study (see 3.3). CanmetENERGY was a sponsoring organization.
- **Measured results:** No specific engine test numbers provided in the source.
- **Source:** Mulard, P.; Schmelzle, P.; Tort, F. 2001. https://osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20209750

### 4.4 Transport Canada – Charge air water injection for NOx reduction (2005)

- **Organization:** Transport Canada, Montreal, Quebec
- **Years:** 2005 (proceedings paper)
- **Engine/vehicle:** Single cylinder water-cooled diesel engine on test bench
- **What was done:** Tested charge air water injection (fumigation) for NOx reduction. Compared single-point vs multi-point injection locations.
- **Measured results:**
  - NOx reduction: 10–35% (most effective at high water injection ratios above 50% engine load)
  - No negative impact on engine operation reported
  - Source paywalled: "Diesel Engine NOx Reduction Using Charge Air Water Injection." *ASME ICEF2005*, pp. 55–66. https://doi.org/10.1115/ICEF2005-1235
- **Notes:** No open full text found; abstract and summary available.
