## 1. Executive summary

Poland has a modest but documented history of water in the combustion cycle research and implementation, spanning from early 20th-century agricultural tractors to modern experimental vehicles and academic research. The most significant documented case is Jan Gulak's water-powered Mercedes W124, which reportedly operates on a 35% fuel / 65% water mixture, with approximately 40 such vehicles operating in Poland. Earlier historical examples include the Ursus C-45 tractor (1947-1959) with intake-manifold water injection for naphtha engines. A Polish patent from 1977-1981 (PL117488B1) describes a method of injecting water into internal combustion engine cylinders. Academic research has been conducted at AGH University of Science and Technology (Kraków), Politechnika Lubelska, Politechnika Poznańska, and Politechnika Śląska, primarily focusing on water-methanol injection, water-in-fuel emulsions, and water injection to reduce knocking and improve combustion efficiency. Commercial water-methanol injection systems are available in Poland from companies such as MTuning.pl, FMIC.pl, and Powertdi.pl.

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles

### Jan Gulak water-powered Mercedes W124 (2003–2026, Poland)

**Organization**: Independent inventor (Jan Gulak, Kielc, Poland)

**Years**: 2003–2026

**Engine/vehicle**: Mercedes-Benz W124, 2.5-liter diesel (OM601), 1986 model

**What was done**: Gulak modified a Mercedes W124 by installing a water injection system where rainwater (collected in a passenger seat tank) is heated in the exhaust manifold to approximately 150–160°C, converting to steam, which is then piped via copper tubing to the intake manifold and directly into the combustion chamber. The system reportedly operates with approximately 65% water and 35% fuel by mass.

**Measured results**: No published measured data in the source I used. According to the inventor, fuel consumption is reduced by about one-third and engine power is increased.

## 5. Historical / racing / aviation-marine transfer (labeled)

### Ursus C-45 tractor with intake-manifold water injection (1947–1959, Poland)

**Organization**: Ursus Factory, Warsaw, Poland

**Years**: 1947–1959

**Vehicle**: Ursus C-45 agricultural tractor (Lanz Bulldog D9506 license-built)

**What was done**: Early Ursus C-45 tractors (1947–1959) featured an intake-manifold water injection system to reduce knocking in naphtha-fueled engines. A water nozzle in the intake manifold sprayed a fine mist of water into the air-fuel mixture. The water vaporized during combustion, cooling the mixture and reducing the risk of pre-ignition and knocking, which was a common problem with naphtha-fueled early engines.

**Measured results**: No measured data in the source I used. The system was a simple but effective solution for reducing knocking in early naphtha-fueled engines.

**Source**:

## Errors and unverified claims in the Hermes draft

- **Jan Gulak / Mercedes W124, 65 % water / 35 % fuel, ~40 vehicles in Poland, fuel use down about one-third.** News-only inventor claims (Motofakty, Fakt, Puls Biznesu). **Not independently measured. Do not use.** A later Polish patent associated with Gulak in secondary lists (e.g. PL207344B1) was not opened in this pass; even if the filing exists, it is not a test map.

- **Ursus C-45 (1947–) intake-manifold water injection for naphtha engines.** The C-45 is a real Polish-built Lanz Bulldog D9506-license **hot-bulb** tractor. Opened tractor sources (including the German Wikibooks entry the draft cited) describe **water jacket cooling** (about 45 L) and **fuel** injection, not combustion-cycle water injection. **Not confirmed. Do not treat C-45 as a production water-injection vehicle.** The draft also wrongly said this case “predates the 1926–2026 period”; 1947 is inside the survey window.

- **PZL-Mielec SW 680 engines** are real diesel engines; the draft itself says there is no water-injection connection. Do not list them as a water-in-combustion case.

- **Gustof, Politechnika Śląska, 2007,** *Transport Problems* paper on combustion temperature versus fuel-injection characteristics in a turbocharged diesel. Real paper record, but it is **not a water-injection study**. Mis-filed.

- **Politechnika Poznańska AVL 5804 laboratory “used for water injection.”** The lab exists; a water-injection research programme was not confirmed from an opened page.

- **Industrial lubricant / demulsifier patents** (PL185217B1 and similar) are out of scope.

- **“No Polish diesel-water emulsion or NOx/PM water-injection research.”** Not independently exhausted in this pass; it remains “nothing confirmed here,” not a proof of absence. The Lublin Wankel work is knock control on hydrogen, not a diesel emulsion map.
