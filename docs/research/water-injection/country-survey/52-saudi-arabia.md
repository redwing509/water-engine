# Saudi Arabia — water in the combustion cycle (checked)

Status: DONE (checked). Hermes drafted (job 20261111-212933-we-survey-52-saudi-arabia-water-in-combu, rc 0, about 8.7 min); the checker re-opened the key sources below on 11 Nov 2026, about 9:45–9:56 PM America/Los_Angeles. No piracy sites were used. The Hermes draft is kept in the private archive only.

Prompt language: Arabic (English-language papers from KAUST, Aramco and Saudi universities also searched — most Saudi engine research is published in English). Report language: English; native titles kept.

## Verification (checker)

**No Saudi production or near-production road vehicle** with water injection, water-methanol injection or water-fuel emulsion was found. Saudi-led **measured** engine work that survived the check is small and all single-cylinder lab work: KAUST port/direct water injection on a single-cylinder SI research engine (2019 SAE paper, 2020 *Energy & Fuels*, Aramco-funded; abstracts only, no numbers), and Jazan University intake water spraying on three single-cylinder engines (2023, open CC BY). Two further Saudi-affiliated measured emulsion studies (PSAU 2025, open abstract; KFUPM 2025, paywalled) and one King Saud University ethanol/water fumigation paper (2015, paywalled) were found that Hermes missed. The PSAU six-stroke work is an **AVL BOOST simulation** only; King Faisal University work is **CHEMKIN modelling** only; Aramco items are **patents** with no measured data. Several items Hermes credited to Saudi Arabia are foreign-led tests with one Saudi co-author. Water is not a fuel in any of these. Searches were not exhaustive (Arabic-language sources only lightly searched).

### Limits

Most KAUST full texts sit behind publisher paywalls and a repository bot check; only abstracts were read, so no KAUST water-injection number is used. The Tianjin *ECM* (251:114969) figures could not be opened. The Hermes draft misattributed two Chinese tests and one Malaysian study to Saudi Arabia, cited the six-stroke review under invented authors, credited a Korean paper to KAUST, and missed three Saudi-affiliated papers, so its other "nothing found" claims are not proven. This note is not proof that no other Saudi measured data exist.
