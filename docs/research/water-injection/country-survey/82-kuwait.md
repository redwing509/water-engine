### Main finding

**Kuwait has one active bench group: the College of Technological Studies, Public Authority for Applied Education and Training (PAAET), testing a 13 %-by-mass water-diesel emulsion in a direct-injection diesel engine on a dynamometer. Fuel use went up; NOx went down. No Kuwaiti production vehicle, product or patent was found.**

- Alardhi, Alrajhi, Alkhulaifi (PAAET), "Investigation of Diesel Engine Performance Using Water Diesel Emulsion", Int. J. Applied Engineering Research 18(1) (2023) 35–41, https://ripublication.com/ijaer23/ijaerv18n1_08.pdf (FULL PDF opened; no DOI printed). Quotes: "water diesel emulsion (WDE) with 13% of water by mass"; "Results showed an increase in brake specific fuel consumption when using WDE compared to AD"; "The BSFC for WDE is higher than that of AD by 8%. As engine speed increases, the variation in BSFC between the two fuels decreases to 5% for both loads" (loads 150 N.m and 200 N.m; BSFC counts diesel mass only); "WDE resulted in reduction of NOx emissions"; conclusion: "The increase in BSFC noted in this work when using WDE means more CO2 into the atmosphere". No NOx percentage for their own engine was found in the opened text (the 14 %, 29 %, 48 % figures in the paper are cited literature, not their results).
- Alardhi, Alrajhi, Alkhulaifi (PAAET), "Investigation of In-cylinder and Fuel Injection Characteristics of Direct Injection Diesel Engine Using Water Diesel Emulsion", J. Mech. Eng. Automation 11(2) (2022), http://article.sapub.org/10.5923.j.jmea.20221102.01.html (abstract and conclusions page via search fetch): cylinder and peak pressures with WDE "close to those observed for AD"; "WDE has lower energy than AD (12% less)"; late start of injection and combustion for WDE; "the lower combustion temperature of WDE is expected to cause a lower NOx emission" (expected, not measured in that paper). DOI-like ID 10.5923/j.jmea.20221102.01 not verified as a registered DOI.
