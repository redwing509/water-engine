## 1. Executive summary

This survey reviewed Mexican water-in-combustion research from 1926–present covering water injection, water-methanol injection, water-fuel emulsions (including diesel-water), steam-assisted cycles, and six-stroke concepts. The search used Spanish and English queries targeting major Mexican institutions (UNAM, IPN, ITESM, Universidad de Guanajuato, Universidad de Guadalajara, INEEL, IMP), OEM plants in Mexico (GM, VW, Nissan, Ford, Stellantis, Honda, Toyota, Kia), historical manufacturers (DINA, Vehículos Automotores Mexicanos), patents (MX, EP, US with Mexican nexus), and academic repositories (SciELO Mexico, tesis.unam.mx, repositorio.ipn.mx, tesis.ipn.mx).

**Key findings:**
- **No documented production or near-production road vehicles** with water-injection systems from Mexican OEM plants or historical Mexican manufacturers (DINA, VAM). OEM Mexico R&D appears focused on assembly/calibration of foreign designs; no local water-injection programs identified in open sources.
- **University and institute work** exists but is limited and often behind institutional access walls. IPN (Instituto Politécnico Nacional) has the most visible thesis work on water injection in internal combustion engines. UNAM repositories returned limited accessible results. ITESM (Tecnológico de Monterrey) shows some research activity but details not fully accessible.
- **IMP (Instituto Mexicano del Petróleo)** work on "inyección de agua" refers to WAG (Water-Alternating-Gas) enhanced oil recovery, not engine combustion water injection.
- **Water-diesel emulsion research** appears in Mexican academic literature but full texts are largely paywalled or institutionally restricted.
- **Patents**: No granted Mexican patents (MX) specifically for engine water injection found in open searches. Some US/EP patents reference Mexican inventors/assignees but none confirmed as Mexican-led engine water-injection IP.
- **Historical/racing/aviation**: No documented Mexican aviation or marine water-injection programs that transferred to road vehicles.

**Overall**: The open-source record shows academic interest (mainly theses and papers) but no production implementation, no verified prototypes from Mexican OEMs, and limited accessible measured data. Most cited sources are abstracts, metadata, or paywalled full texts.

## 9. Limits and open questions

1. **Institutional access barrier**: The majority of Mexican academic work (IPN, UNAM, ITESM theses and papers) is behind institutional repository walls. Without university credentials, measured data cannot be verified. This is the single largest gap.
2. **Patent database access**: IMPI (Mexican patent office) does not offer a freely searchable full-text database via open web. Google Patents coverage of MX patents is incomplete. Mexican-owned engine water-injection patents may exist but are not discoverable via open tools.
3. **OEM opacity**: Mexican OEM plants are production/calibration centers; R&D is typically centralized in home countries (US, Germany, Japan, Korea, France). Local water-injection work, if any, would be confidential.
4. **Language and indexing**: Spanish-language papers may be indexed in regional databases (Redalyc, Dialnet, Latindex) not fully covered by standard web search. Some may have open PDFs there.
5. **CONACYT project database**: No open, queryable database of funded projects found for "inyección de agua motor". The CONACYT CVU/SNI researcher directories exist but require name-based search.
6. **Aftermarket / tuning scene**: Mexican tuners may use US water-methanol kits (Snow, AEM, CoolingMist) but no published Mexican dyno studies or technical papers found.
7. **Historical gap (1926–1990s)**: No open sources found for early Mexican engine research on water injection. DINA/VAM archives not digitized publicly.
8. **Collaboration with foreign labs**: Some ITESM/IPN abstracts hint at collaboration with US/EU labs (e.g., UW-Madison ERC, Sandia, TU Darmstadt) but no joint publications with measured data found open.

**Recommendation for deeper study**: Obtain institutional access (or author contact) for IPN/UNAM/ITESM theses; search Redalyc/Dialnet/Latindex for open Mexican engineering papers; query IMPI directly via their official search if available; contact IMP automotive division (if any) for combustion-related work distinct from petroleum recovery.
