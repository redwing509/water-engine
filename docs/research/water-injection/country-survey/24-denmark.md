## 1. Executive summary

Denmark has a significant history of water injection research primarily through its major marine engine manufacturers, particularly MAN Energy Solutions (historically Burmeister & Wain/B&W) and Holeby Diesel. The research has focused mainly on marine and stationary applications rather than road vehicles. Key findings include:

- Danish companies have developed water-in-fuel emulsion technology for marine engines
- Several Danish patents exist for water injection systems, primarily for large two-stroke engines
- Collaborative projects between Danish companies and research institutions have yielded promising NOx reduction results
- Most Danish work has been focused on marine/industrial applications rather than automotive

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles

No documented cases of water injection systems in production or near-production road vehicles from Danish manufacturers. Danish automotive water injection research appears to be limited or non-existent in the public domain.

## 3. Research, prototypes, and supplier systems

### 3.1 MAN Energy Solutions (B&W) Water Injection Systems

**Organization:** MAN Energy Solutions, Danish branch (formerly Burmeister & Wain/B&W)
**Years:** 2006-2020s
**Engine/vehicle:** Large two-stroke marine engines (industrial/marine)
**What was done:** Development of direct water injection (DWI) systems for large marine diesel engines. Danish patents include:
- DK179162B1: "An internal combustion engine of two-stroke crosshead type, and a method of direct injection of fuel and water into a combustion chamber"
- DK177782B1: "Internal combustion engine and a water-in-fuel emulsion creation and injection pump for it"
- DK181629B1: "Large two-stroke uniflow scavenged internal combustion engine configured to operate with high jacket water temperature"

**Measured results:** NOx reductions of 50-60% achievable with direct water injection without power derating. Water/fuel ratios of approximately 0.45 for generator mode and 0.55 for propulsion mode.

### 3.2 Danisco-MAN Diesel & Turbo Collaborative Project

**Organization:** Danisco (now part of DuPont) and MAN Diesel & Turbo
**Years:** 2009-2011
**Engine/vehicle:** Marine diesel engines
**What was done:** Joint project funded by Danish Environmental Protection Agency to develop stable water-in-fuel emulsions for distillate fuels. Tested on MAN B&W Diesel 4T50ME-X four-cylinder two-stroke test engine in Copenhagen.

**Measured results:** NOx emission reductions approaching 60% at 50% load with 50% water content emulsions. CO and soot emissions also reduced. Project successfully identified emulsifiers capable of stabilizing large quantities of water in distillate fuels.

## 4. University and institute work

### 4.1 DTU (Technical University of Denmark)

**Years:** 2010s-2020s
**Research areas:**

**Notable work:** Studies on urea and water injection effects on particle number concentration across SCR catalysts in Euro VI diesel engines.

## 5. Historical / racing / aviation-marine transfer (labeled)

## 6. Water-fuel emulsions and other cycles

### 6.1 Danish Water-in-Fuel Emulsion Technology

**Organization:** Danisco and MAN Diesel & Turbo collaboration
**Years:** 2009-2011
**Technology:** Water-in-fuel emulsions (WIF) for marine engines
**What was done:** Development of emulsifiers capable of stabilizing up to 50% water in distillate marine fuels. Tested on 4T50ME-X engine in Copenhagen.

**Results:** 
- NOx reductions approaching 60% at 50% load
- CO and soot emissions also reduced
- Stable emulsions achieved with suitable emulsifiers
- Project documented in Danish Environmental Protection Agency report No. 1380 (2011)

## Confirmed (spot-checked)

- **No Danish OEM series-production passenger car or commercial road vehicle** with factory combustion-cycle water injection was found.

- **Andreasen (MAN Diesel & Turbo) and Nyggard (Danisco), Danish EPA Environmental Project No. 1380 (2011),** *Water-in-fuel emulsion as marine engine fuel for reduced NOx and particulate emissions.* Official open PDF: https://www2.mst.dk/udgiv/publications/2011/08/978-87-92779-30-4.pdf ; landing page https://mst.dk/publikationer/2011/september/water-in-fuel-emulsion-as-marine-engine-fuel-for-reduced-nox-and-particulate-emissions . The preface states that **all MAN Diesel & Turbo activity in this project was located in Denmark**. Engine tests were run on the **B&W 4T50ME-X** four-cylinder two-stroke research engine at MAN Diesel & Turbo in **Copenhagen**. In the engine chapters, “water content” means **water/fuel** (water added relative to the fuel amount), not water/(water+fuel). Official English summary and §4.3: NOx (g/kWh) is **approaching 60 %** lower “at the highest water content applied at **50 % load**.” The report does **not**, in that sentence, name that highest water content as 50 %. WIF also **reduces CO** and **filter smoke number (soot)**, while **HC** is somewhat higher than the reference and **ISO 8178 dilution-tunnel particulate mass increases** with added water (the report attributes this in part to a longer injection and a high soluble-organic fraction; elemental carbon is described as a tiny share of total PM). SFOC generally increases at larger water additions; a previous estimate in the report is about **1.8 %** (and 2.8 % in an earlier evaporation-only estimate) SFOC penalty at **30 vol. %** added water, with a slight SFOC improvement at moderate water at 25 % and 50 % load. Combined **WIF + EGR** (preliminary, not long-term ship-tested): NOx **down to 0.2 g/kWh** (reductions **well above 90 %**, in some cases **above 98 %**), with a significant SFOC penalty and ISO PM rising from **0.06 to 0.10 g/kWh** in the case quoted. Example at 75 % load: WIF **50 vol. %** added water with **37 % EGR**. **Marine / laboratory two-stroke, not a road vehicle.**

- **DK179162B1** is a real Danish patent record (MAN Diesel & Turbo Filial af MAN Diesel & Turbo SE, Tyskland; inventors include Mayer, Sjöholm, Kjemtrup; 2017) for water supplied to a liquefied-gas system and/or directly to a liquid-gas injector on a two-stroke crosshead engine. **Patent, not a measured map.** Do not use the draft’s **50–60 % NOx** or water/fuel **0.45 (generator) / 0.55 (propulsion)** figures as measured MAN DWI results; those ratios were not found in the opened EPA report and resemble published Wärtsilä DWI descriptions already used in the Finland report.

- **DK181629B1** (high jacket-water temperature) is **ordinary jacket cooling**, out of scope.

- **Prasath, Bernemyr, and Erlandsson, SAE 2020-01-2196 / DTU Orbit,** *On the Effects of Urea and Water Injection on Particles Across the SCR Catalyst in a Heavy-Duty Euro VI Diesel Engine.* Official abstract: deionized water and AdBlue were injected **into the SCR aftertreatment unit**, not into the combustion cycle. **Out of scope** (urea/DEF / exhaust aftertreatment). DOI 10.4271/2020-01-2196 ; https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/on-the-effects-of-urea-and-water-injection-on-particles-across-th/

- Holeby Diesel history with no documented water-injection programme is consistent with “nothing found” for that firm. Early B&W “developed water injection systems” from 1898 is **not verified** in an opened official source.

## Errors and unverified claims in the Hermes draft

- **“Approaching 60 % NOx at 50 % load with 50 % water.”** Official text: approaching 60 % at the **highest water content applied at 50 % load**; that content is not named as 50 % in the summary sentence. 50 vol. % added water appears in a **combined WIF+EGR** example at **75 % load**.

- **“CO and soot also reduced” without the PM caveat.** Official: CO and FSN down; ISO 8178 PM **up**.

- **MAN DWI “50–60 % NOx” and WFR 0.45 / 0.55.** Not taken from an opened measured MAN DWI report; possible mix with Wärtsilä manufacturer figures.

- **DK181629B1 jacket water** treated as combustion-cycle water injection.

- **DTU SAE 2020-01-2196** treated as engine water injection; it is SCR-catalyst water/urea injection.

- Unverified historical B&W water-injection development from the early diesel era.
