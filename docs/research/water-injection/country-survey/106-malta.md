### Main finding

**University of Malta (Department of Mechanical Engineering, Mario Farrugia's group) has measured water-injection engine work: a 2014–2015 SI port water-injection bench and a 2026 diesel–LPG dual-fuel water vs AdBlue port-injection knock study.** No production vehicle, product or patent was found.

- Busuttil, Farrugia, "Experimental investigation on the effect of injecting water to the air to fuel mixture in a spark ignition engine", MM Science Journal, March 2015 ([UM OAR PDF](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/133385/1/Experimental%20investigation%20on%20the%20effect%20of%20injecting%20water%20to%20the%20air%20to%20fuel%20mixture%20in%20a%20spark%20ignition%20engine.pdf); [doi:10.17973/mmsj.2015_03_201510](https://doi.org/10.17973/mmsj.2015_03_201510), resolves). Affiliation: "University of Malta, Msida Malta".
  - "Optimum water injection quantities were found to be about 0.015 ml to 0.031 ml of water per cycle on a 592 cc SI engine."
  - "Maximum obtained torque improvement with water injection was 16 %. This was achieved at a manifold absolute pressure of 120 kPa, with air temperature at ambient. The same load condition, 120 kPa, with air heated to the temperature that would be obtained from isentropic compression, resulted in a torque improvement of 7 %."
  - "an increase of 16 % in torque is noticeable from a fuel to water injection ratio by volume of 4.65."
  - "Water did not suppress knock at 120 kPa loading."
- Busuttil, Camilleri, Farrugia (University of Malta), "Mechatronics for water injection in SI engine", Mechatronika 2014 ([doi:10.1109/mechatronika.2014.7018276](https://doi.org/10.1109/mechatronika.2014.7018276), resolves; abstract via OpenAlex): "Water injection electronic circuitry was designed and built from scratch" on "an older single-cylinder JAP engine". This is the control hardware for the bench above.
- Scicluna, Farrugia, "AdBlue Port Injection for Dual-Fuel Compression-Ignition Engine Knock Suppression", Energies, published 2 March 2026 ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/5/1242); [doi:10.3390/en19051242](https://doi.org/10.3390/en19051242), resolves). "Department of Mechanical Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, University of Malta".
  - "Experiments were performed on a 2.0 L diesel–LPG engine operated at 2000 RPM, BMEP ≈ 9 bar, λ ≈ 1.27 and LPG substitution of 72%."
  - "WPI at a water mass ratio WMR of 130% reduced the peak HRR by 56% and mean KISE by 88%, but decreased the peak pressure, BMEP and BTE."
  - "API at an AdBlue mass ratio AMR of 130% reduced the peak HRR by 37% and KISE by 82.6% while maintaining BMEP and BTE within baseline variability."
  - "NOx emissions decreased with WPI but increased at a high AMR."
  - AdBlue is a urea-water solution. It is included here only as the comparison arm and is not treated as a fuel.
