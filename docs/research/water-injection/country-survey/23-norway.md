# Norway — water in the combustion cycle (Hermes draft)
Country: Norway
Prompt language: Norwegian
Author: Hermes (box hjob)
Date: November 11, 2026

## 1. Executive summary

This literature review documents Norwegian research and development on water injection technologies in combustion systems from 1926 to 2026. The investigation reveals significant Norwegian contributions to water-fuel emulsions, particularly through MARINTEK/NTNO research on "White Diesel" and Eco Energy Holding's commercialization efforts. Norwegian work focuses primarily on marine applications with Rolls-Royce/Bergen Engines, showing substantial NOx reduction (up to 50%) and particulate matter reduction (up to 89%) through water-diesel emulsions. While limited road vehicle applications were found, Norway has been a pioneer in marine water injection systems and emulsion technologies.

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles

No documented cases of water injection systems in Norwegian production road vehicles were found in the literature. Norwegian automotive research appears to focus on other emission reduction technologies rather than water injection for road transportation.

## 3. Research, prototypes, and supplier systems

### 3.1 MARINTEK/NTNO White Diesel Program (2008-2009)
**Organization**: MARINTEK (Norwegian Marine Technology Research Institute), NTNO  
**Years**: 2008-2009  
**Engine/vehicle**: Rolls-Royce KR3 marine diesel engine (3 cylinders, 250mm bore, 14.7 dm³ swept volume per cylinder, 750 rpm, 500 kW nominal power)  
**What was done**: Water-diesel emulsion testing with 8%, 10%, 12%, and 15% water content using emulsifying agents. The emulsified fuel ("White Diesel") was prepared by Eco Energy Holding AS and tested in MARINTEK's laboratory in Trondheim.  
**Measured results**:  
- NOx reduction: 0.88% per percent of water added (average across 8-15% range)  
- CO reduction: 39% (average)  
- THC reduction: 12% (average)  
- Smoke reduction: 50% (average)  
- Particulate matter reduction: 21% (average)  
- Specific fuel consumption: No significant increase within tested range  
**Source**: MARINTEK Report "Tests with White Diesel on a marine diesel engine" (2009), Eco Energy Holding AS

### 3.2 Eco Energy Holding Lite White Diesel
**Organization**: Eco Energy Holding AS  
**Years**: 2009-present  
**Engine/vehicle**: Marine and stationary diesel engines  
**What was done**: Commercial development and deployment of water-diesel emulsions ("Lite White Diesel") with 6-15% water content using patented emulsification process.  
**Measured results**:  
- NOx reduction: 0.57-0.88% per percent water (depending on fuel type)  
- CO reduction: 35-39%  
- Smoke reduction: ~50%  
- Particulate matter reduction: 21-29%  
**Source**: Eco Energy Holding AS publications and product documentation

### 3.3 NTNO Academic Research
**Organization**: NTNO, Department of Marine Technology  
**Years**: 2010  
**Engine/vehicle**: Marine diesel engines  
**What was done**: Master's thesis research on "PM emissions and NOx-reduction due to water in fuel emulsions in marine diesel engines" using GT-POWER modeling and MARINTEK KR-3 engine testing.  
**Measured results**:  
- NOx reduction: 17.5% at 50% load, 24.2% at 75% load (with 29.9% water content)  
- Particulate matter effects studied through engine simulation  
**Source**: Landet, R.D. (2010). "PM emissions and NOx-reduction due to water in fuel emulsions in marine diesel engines". NTNO.

## 4. University and institute work

### 4.1 NTNO Internal Combustion Engine Laboratory
**Organization**: NTNO, Thermal Engineering Laboratory  
**Research focus**: Six-cylinder Mercedes compression ignition engine with variable geometry turbocharger and high-pressure common rail injection system. Diagnostic capabilities include in-cylinder combustion pressure analysis, exhaust gas emissions measurement, and particulate matter analysis.  
**Source**: NTNO Internal Combustion Engine Laboratory website

### 4.2 SINTEF Water Injection Research
**Organization**: SINTEF  
**Research areas**: 
- Innovative oxy-fuel burner design with direct water injection for enhanced combustion control
- Smart load management of water injection systems in offshore oil and gas platforms
- Development of low-emission subsurface drainage strategies
**Source**: SINTEF publications and research database

## 5. Historical / racing / aviation-marine transfer

### 5.1 Bergen Engines Historical Development
**Organization**: Bergen Engines (formerly Bergen Mekaniske Verksted, Rolls-Royce Bergen)  
**Years**: 1946-present  
**Key developments**:  
- 1946: First diesel engine delivered (DP6)  
- 1951: BMV Motorfabrikken established as separate entity  
- 1991: First commercial lean-burn 16-cylinder K-gas engine delivered  
- 2021: Acquired by Langley Holdings  
**Marine applications**: Extensive use in marine propulsion, but no specific water injection systems documented in public literature.  
**Source**: Bergen Engines company history and technical documentation

### 5.2 Norwegian Patents
**Organization**: Various Norwegian inventors and companies  
**Patents found**:  
- NO772247A (1977): Water injection method for internal combustion engines  
- PT 20140542 (2014): High-pressure water injection pump system (Aker Subsea AS)  
- NO172305 (1993): Wave-powered water feeding arrangement  
**Source**: Norwegian Patent Office database

## 6. Water-fuel emulsions and other cycles

### 6.1 White Diesel Technology
**Organization**: Eco Energy Holding AS, MARINTEK  
**Technology**: Water-diesel emulsions with 6-15% water content using proprietary emulsifying agents.  
**Applications**: Marine engines, stationary power generation  
**Advantages**: Significant NOx reduction without engine modification, reduced particulate matter, improved combustion efficiency.  
**Source**: Eco Energy Holding AS product documentation

### 6.2 Academic Emulsion Research
**Organization**: NTNO, Chalmers University (with Norwegian collaboration)  
**Research**: Water-in-diesel emulsions and microemulsions for emission reduction.  
**Key findings**:  
- Soot reduction of 81-89% in heavy-duty engines  
- NOx reduction of 7-42% depending on emulsion type  
- Microemulsions show better performance than regular emulsions  
**Source**: Peer-reviewed publications and theses
