### Official Egyptian maps that can be used (with the comparisons they actually state)

- Official Ali M.A. Attia (Benha University, corresponding) and A.R. Kulchitskiy (Vladimir State University), “Influence of the structure of water-in-fuel emulsion on diesel engine performance,” *Fuel* 116 (2014) 703–708, DOI 10.1016/j.fuel.2013.08.057. Official Benha University staff copy opened: https://beng.stafpu.bu.edu.eg/Mechanical%20Engineering/4109/publications/Ali%20Mahmoud%20Ali%20Atya_Fuel%20Vol%20116%20pp%20703-708%202014.pdf. Official abstract: turbocharged three-cylinder diesel; membrane emulsification at 0.2 µm and 0.45 µm; engine tests at 17% water by volume and 0.5% emulsifier (the body also states that the engine ran on two WFE samples with 17% volumetric water and 0.5% emulsifier). Official abstract: emulsions with larger water droplets reduced NOx by up to 25%; emulsions with finer droplets reduced smoke by more than 80% and unburned hydrocarbons by more than 35%, and increased effective engine efficiency by up to 20%. Official conclusions confirm effective efficiency up to 1.2 times that on diesel (the same 20% figure) and that large droplets affect NOx more while smaller droplets affect hydrocarbons and smoke; they do not restate the 25 / 80 / 35 percentages. A two-column body region also lists a 79.75% diesel + 19.75% water + 0.5% emulsifier label; that is not taken as the engine-test mixture, because the official abstract and the body engine-test sentence both state 17% water.

- Official M. Nour (Farag), H. Kosaka, Ali K. Abdel-Rahman, and M. Bady, “Effect of Water Injection into Exhaust Manifold on Diesel Engine Combustion and Emissions,” *Energy Procedia* 100 (2016) 178–187, DOI 10.1016/j.egypro.2016.10.162 (CC BY-NC-ND). Official Benha University staff copy opened: https://beng.stafpu.bu.edu.eg/Mechanical%20Engineering/4117/publications/Mohamed%20Nour%20_Effect_of_Water_Injection_into_Exhaust_Manifold_on.pdf. Affiliations: Tokyo Institute of Technology and Energy Resources Engineering, Egypt-Japan University of Science and Technology (E-JUST), Alexandria; corresponding email at E-JUST. Experimental single-cylinder diesel. Method: water is injected into the exhaust manifold and, by opening the exhaust valve during the intake stroke, the water and exhaust gas are reinducted into the cylinder (humidified internal EGR), not port or direct water injection. Official conclusions: IMEP is 4.1% and 14% lower at 10% and 25% EGR than conventional diesel; with water injection, IMEP rises by 2.6% and 2.7% (350° and 400° timing) at 10% EGR, and by 11% and 11.4% at 25% EGR, compared with EGR without water injection (the official abstract’s “14%” and “11%” match the 25% EGR case and did not state that EGR ratio). Official abstract: NOx reduced by up to 85% versus conventional diesel **due to the EGR effect**. Official conclusions: NOx decreased by 40%, 80%, and 85% at 10%, 25%, and 30% EGR; those conclusion bullets do not separate water injection from EGR for NOx. Do not take 85% as a water-injection-only map. Official abstract: soot reduced by up to 40% with water injection versus EGR without water, but still higher than conventional diesel. Official conclusions: soot can be decreased by 45% with water injection at the same EGR condition. Because the official abstract and conclusions disagree on the soot percentage (40% versus 45%), that soot figure is not taken as settled.

- Official Mohamed Farag, Hidenori Kosaka, Mahmoud Bady, and Ali K. Abdel-Rahman, “Effects of intake and exhaust manifold water injection on combustion and emission characteristics of a DI diesel engine,” *Journal of Thermal Science and Technology* 12(1) 2017, Paper No. 17-00067, DOI 10.1299/jtst.2017jtst0014. Official J-STAGE PDF opened: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jtst/12/1/12_2017jtst0014/_pdf. Affiliations: E-JUST (corresponding); Tokyo Institute of Technology; Assiut University; Islamic University of Medina (Bady). Experimental DI diesel; 40 mg/cycle of water into the intake or exhaust manifold, with 10% and 25% EGR. Official abstract and body agree that exhaust-manifold water injection at 25% EGR reduced BSFC by about 5% versus EGR at 25% without water, and that indicated thermal efficiency improved by 3% for exhaust-manifold water injection versus EGR without water. Official abstract IMEP “+7%” at that peak-improvement point was not restated in the conclusions list and is labeled here as official-abstract only. Official body and abstract agree that exhaust-manifold water injection at 10% EGR reduced soot by 55% versus EGR without water. Official abstract “88%” NOx reduction for intake-manifold water injection is not restated in the conclusions and does not match the official body comparisons (NOx versus the matching EGR case: −16% for EGR10 exhaust-WI, −56% for EGR10 intake-WI, −41% for EGR25 intake-WI, and +5% for EGR25 exhaust-WI; EGR alone already reduced NOx by 52% and 85% at 10% and 25% EGR versus conventional diesel). The 88% figure is not taken as settled. A body sentence that exhaust-manifold WI at 10% EGR “enhanced the bsfc by 19%” disagrees in sign with the official conclusions (“increased the bsfc by 19%” at EGR10); the 19% figure is not taken.
