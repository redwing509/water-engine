Country: United Kingdom

## 1. Executive summary
The United Kingdom has a deep, continuous history of water-in-combustion research spanning from Harry Ricardo’s pre-WWI knock investigations through WWII aero-engine anti-detonant injection (ADI) to modern downsized, boosted GDI engines. No UK production road car has shipped with factory water injection. The closest to production was the ULTRABOOST collaborative project (2010-2013) led by Jaguar Land Rover with University of Bath, Imperial College London, University of Leeds, Lotus Engineering, and MAHLE Powertrain, which demonstrated 5-15% net indicated efficiency gains and 60% NOx reduction with port and direct water injection on a 2.0 L highly boosted engine. Aftermarket water-methanol systems have been supplied by UK firms (notably Aquamist at Peacehaven) since the 1990s for motorsport and tuning. University research at Brunel, Brighton, Bath, Leeds, and Imperial has produced peer-reviewed data on water-diesel emulsions, micro-explosion phenomena, and water injection in GDI and dual-fuel engines. Ford UK (Dunton) holds recent patents for direct water injection in EcoBoost engines but has not deployed them in production. Aviation-to-road transfer occurred via Ricardo’s WWII Merlin ADI work and the Meteor tank engine, but no direct Merlin-water-injection road-car derivative reached series production.

## 9. Limits and open questions
- **Production deployment gap**: Despite strong consortium results (ULTRABOOST), no UK OEM has put water injection into series production. Open question: water tank packaging, consumer acceptance (refilling), corrosion/warranty, and cost/benefit vs. cooled EGR and higher-octane fuel.
- **Water-diesel emulsion commercialisation**: Brunel/Brighton work shows consistent NOx/PM reduction but BSFC penalty, HC/CO increase, and unregulated carbonyl rise at low load. Surfactant cost and long-term stability remain barriers. No UK engine maker has adopted.
- **Ford UK patents** (2016-2018) suggest direct water injection hardware ready for EcoBoost; why not deployed? Possibly superseded by 48 V mild-hybrid and Miller-cycle strategies.
- **Aquamist aftermarket**: No independent, peer-reviewed validation of current HFS4-v3.1 system on modern GDI engines found. Open question: real-world knock suppression and efficiency on pump fuel vs. marketing claims.
- **Six-stroke / steam cycles**: No UK activity found. Global patent landscape (Crower, US) not transferred to UK labs.
- **Aviation-to-road transfer**: Merlin ADI proved water injection at extreme power density (2,640 hp / 27 L); Meteor tank engine dropped ADI. No modern UK road engine uses ADI. Question: could ADI enable downsized high-output road engines (e.g., 400 kW/L) with acceptable water logistics?
- **Data access**: Several key SAE/Journal papers remain paywalled without legitimate open copies. Author preprints (Bath, Leeds, Brunel, Brighton) partially fill the gap but not all numbers are available.

## Confirmed (spot-checked)

- **No UK production passenger car with combustion-cycle water injection.** Negative results for Jaguar XJ220, TVR, Lotus, Aston Martin, Bentley, Rolls-Royce cars, McLaren, Mini, and Ford UK production models are accepted for this pass. The XJ220 used intercooling, not water injection.

- **ULTRABOOST (2010–2013) is a downsizing project, not a water-injection programme.** SAE **2014-01-1185**, Turner et al., *Ultra Boost for Economy: Extending the Limits of Extreme Engine Downsizing*, DOI https://doi.org/10.4271/2014-01-1185. A legitimate open accepted manuscript is on White Rose: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/79226/ and https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/79226/1/Burluka%202014-01-1185.pdf . The open abstract describes a ~60 % capacity cut aiming at the JLR 5.0 L V8 torque curve (including **25 bar BMEP at 1,000 rpm**), compound charging, direct *and* port **fuel** injection, variable valvetrain and cam phasing — **not water injection**. Partners in the author list include JLR, University of Bath (Akehurst, Brace, Copeland, Lewis), Imperial (Martinez-Botas, Romagnoli), Leeds (Burluka), Lotus, MAHLE, Shell. **Do not attach +5 % / +15 % efficiency or −60 % NOx to this paper or to the 2010–2013 project as such.** SAE 2014-01-1196 and 2014-01-1397 were not opened; they should not be used as water-injection sources either.

- **University of Bath water injection on the ULTRABOOST *engine*, later papers.**
  - Golzari et al., *Impact of intake port injection of water on boosted downsized gasoline direct injection engine combustion, efficiency and emissions*, *Int. J. Engine Research*, 2019, DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/1468087419832791 . Open-abstract figures used here (journal PDF paywalled; **no Bath repository preprint was found in this pass**): **port** water injection; net indicated efficiency improved by up to about **5 % at medium load** and **15 % at high load**; **NOx reduced by up to about 60 %**; hydrocarbons increased at higher water/fuel ratios.   - Akehurst et al., *An Experimental Study of Water Injection in a Downsized, Highly Boosted Direct Injection Spark Ignition Engine*, **28th Aachen Colloquium, 2019** (not 2013). - **Rolls-Royce Merlin ADI / RM.17SM (aviation).** An experimental Merlin 100-series development (RM.17SM) was tested at Derby around 1944 at very high boost with **150-octane fuel and water injection**, with a widely quoted figure of **2,640 hp at 36 lb/sq in** for a short run (about 15 minutes in later accounts). It was not a production rating. **Do not use “vs ~1,600 hp without water injection”** — that is not a like-for-like baseline for the same test, and production Merlins already exceeded 1,600 hp on other ratings. Label all of this aviation. The Meteor tank engine as a de-rated Merlin without ADI is consistent with secondary histories; not used as combustion-cycle data.

- **Harry Ricardo’s historical knock / anti-detonant work** is real in outline (books, Royal Society memoir). Specific BMEP “168 psi” and similar figures were not extracted from an opened primary text in this pass.
