# Portugal — water in the combustion cycle (Hermes draft)
Country: Portugal
Prompt language: Portuguese
Author: Hermes (box hjob)
Date: 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary

This survey covers Portuguese academic and industrial research on water injection in combustion engines for road vehicles, from approximately 1926 to 2026. Twenty-one targeted web searches were conducted across Portuguese university repositories (UL/IST, UP/FEUP/INEGI, UC, UMinho, UAveiro, UBI, IPL), patent databases, and industry sources (Bosch Portugal, LNEG). The search used both Portuguese ("injeção de água", "emulsão água-diesel", "emulsão água-gasóleo", "emulsão gasóleo-água", "emulsão diesel-água", "água no cilindro") and English ("water injection", "water in cylinder", "water-diesel emulsion") terms.

**Key findings:** Portugal has a modest but measurable body of academic work on water injection and water-diesel emulsions, concentrated in university research groups at Instituto Superior Técnico (IST/UL), Universidade da Beira Interior (UBI), Instituto Politécnico de Leiria (IPL), and FEUP/INEGI (Porto). Industrial research at Bosch Portugal (Aveiro/Braga) focuses on heat pumps and water heating, not engine water injection. LNEG (Laboratório Nacional de Energia e Geologia) appears in general energy research but no specific engine water-injection publications were located in open sources. No production or near-production road-vehicle water-injection systems were found in Portugal. The documented cases are laboratory research, student theses, and Formula Student/Shell Eco-marathon prototype work (labeled as motorsport prototypes). Water-in-diesel emulsion research is the most active sub-topic. Spark-ignition water injection appears under-represented in open Portuguese sources.

## 2. Production or near-production road vehicles

**No documented cases.** No Portuguese OEM or supplier has brought a water-injection system to series production or near-production for road vehicles. Bosch Portugal develops and manufactures automotive sensors, infotainment, and hot-water/heat-pump systems in Aveiro and Braga, but its Portuguese R&D output in open sources shows no engine water-injection projects for road vehicles.
