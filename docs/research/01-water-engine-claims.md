# 01 — Historical claims of "engines that run on water"

> Status: research dossier, compiled October 2026. Every factual statement below is attributed to a cited source. Where a source is weak (single outlet, self-published, forum, proponent archive), that is stated explicitly. Inclusion in this list is **not** an allegation of wrongdoing; convictions, fines, or fraud findings are mentioned **only** where a cited source explicitly reports them.

## Introduction

Water (H₂O) is what you get *after* hydrogen has burned: it is fully oxidised hydrogen, at the bottom of the chemical-energy hill. Burning hydrogen releases about 286 kJ per mole of liquid water formed (higher heating value); splitting that water back into H₂ and O₂ needs at least the same energy (at least ~237 kJ/mol as electrical work, the rest as heat), and every real electrolyser, alternator, and engine adds losses on top. So a closed "water → hydrogen → engine → electricity → electrolysis" loop always runs downhill, and water alone can't be a net fuel. Every historical "water engine" therefore falls into one of a few buckets:

1. **A hidden or unacknowledged conventional fuel.** Examples are petrol in the mix, or a petrol tank that was never disconnected.
2. **A consumable chemical reactant that is the real fuel.** Examples are calcium carbide, aluminium, or metal hydrides. Water is only a co-reactant.
3. **Onboard electrolysis ("HHO"/"Brown's gas") powered by the battery or alternator.** It is a net energy loss.
4. **Misreporting of a legitimate technology.** Examples are hydrogen fuel cells, hydrogen IC engines, water injection, six-stroke engines, and solar water-splitting.

This dossier documents the claims as they were made and reported, with deep coverage of **India** first, then the most notable global cases.

# Part A — India: claims

## 1. Ramar Pillai — "herbal petrol" (Tamil Nadu, 1996–2016)

- **Who / where:** Ramar Pillai, a school dropout from the Rajapalayam / Idaiyankulam area (Virudhunagar district), Tamil Nadu [1][2][3].
- **Claim:** He would boil leaves and bark in water, cool the mixture, then add salt, citric acid (lime juice) and unspecified chemicals. A combustible "herbal fuel" would separate out within about 30 minutes [1][4]. At a 16 Sept 1996 demonstration in the Tamil Nadu Secretariat, attended by Chief Minister M. Karunanidhi and Union ministers, two litres of water with herbal extract, salt and lime juice were used [2]. Coverage widely framed it as "petrol from water" [2][10].
- **Early official reaction:** A Sept 1996 Indian Express/PTI report (archived by IISc) says an IIT chemist organised a New Delhi demonstration at the request of the Department of Science and Technology (DST). The DST secretary repeated it and said "we have no doubt that we are sitting on something very big." IIT Madras static engine tests reportedly gave good results, and DST funded a 300-litre/day pilot plant. The same report notes the product smelled like kerosene, distilled at about 170 °C, and that scientists "could not explain where the carbon came from" [4]. Tamil Nadu offered land and support [2][3].
- **Independent tests / expert assessment:**
  - Later tests at IIT and the Indian Institute of Petroleum (IIP), Dehradun, were reported as failures [1][2]. A Kochi demonstration in Nov 1998 failed to convince attending chemists [6].
  - A parliamentary committee relying on IIP called the discovery a hoax and described the product as hydrocarbons boiled in kerosene with dissolved camphor. Pillai disputed this [5].
  - The South China Morning Post reported the head of DST saying lab-controlled tests showed Pillai was adding petrol and paraffin, and that a stirring spoon he insisted on using was found hollowed out and filled with petrol. A DST statement tabled in Parliament labelled him a "fraud" [7].
  - The DST secretary testified in court (2001) that IIP analysis showed Pillai did not produce the claimed herbal fuel [8].
- **Legal outcome:** The CBI arrested Pillai on 11 March 2000, alleging he cheated the public by selling stolen/industrial chemicals as motor fuel. More than 10,000 litres of "Ramar Herbal Fuel" were seized [1][2][9]. On 13 Oct 2016 a Chennai magistrate's court (Egmore) convicted Pillai and four associates of cheating. Each received **three years' rigorous imprisonment**, with a total fine of Rs 30,000; the CBI said the group gained about Rs 2.27 crore [3]. Gulf Times (citing the court) reported he mixed petroleum products and marketed them as herbal fuel [10]. **No reporting of an appeal outcome was found.**
- **Likely real energy source:** Petroleum-derived hydrocarbons (petrol / kerosene / industrial solvents), per the lab findings and the court [3][7][10].

## 2. Sounthirajan Kumarasamy — "distilled-water engine" (Coimbatore, 2019)

- **Date/place correction:** The brief referred to a "Prof. S. Kumaraswamy, Chennai, ~2017." The well-documented case is **Sounthirajan (Soundararajan) Kumarasamy, a mechanical engineer in Coimbatore**, with national coverage in **May 2019** [1][2][3]. No separate Chennai/2017 professor claim was found.
- **Claim:** A "Super Sonic Hydrogen IC Engine" that runs on distilled water, "uses hydrogen as the fuel source and releases oxygen." He reported about 10 years of work, a 100 cc design, and "200 km on 10 litres of distilled water" [2][3]. He said Indian authorities did not respond, while the Japanese government had, and that the engine would launch in Japan "in a few days" [2][3]. He said a patent was granted in Nov 2018, though no patent number was reported [3].
- **Independent tests / expert assessment:** None found. Coverage (ANI-sourced) quotes only the inventor [1][2][3]. Wikipedia's article likewise lists only press coverage, with no verification [1].
- **Outcome:** No documented Japanese launch or government statement was found. In Sept 2019 he reported an anonymous threat letter to police [4].
- **Physics note:** If the engine splits water and burns the hydrogen, it consumes from the air exactly as much oxygen as electrolysis released. Net "oxygen release" is impossible unless hydrogen is *not* burned. Any net work would require an external energy input (battery, fuel, or consumable reactant).
- **Likely real energy source:** Undisclosed. It must be electrical (battery) or chemical (hidden fuel or reactant).

## 3. Raees (Mohammad Raees Mahmood) Makrani — "water car" (Sagar, Madhya Pradesh, 2005–2016)

- **Claim:** A car mechanic in Sadar Bazaar, Sagar, modified an 800 cc petrol engine. He began in 2005 and reported success in 2012. Media described the result as a car that "runs on water" [1][2][3].
- **Actual mechanism (by his own account):** **Acetylene gas generated onboard by reacting calcium carbide with water** (CaC₂ + 2H₂O → C₂H₂ + Ca(OH)₂). He said "it is just about the water," and cited costs of about Rs 10–20 per litre-equivalent [1][2][3].
- **Outcome:** He reportedly filed a patent in 2013, and Hindi-language coverage says it was later granted (no number given) [3]. Coverage reports Chinese and Dubai investor interest that he declined [1][3]. No independent tests or commercial product were found. Cartoq's explainer stresses that the car runs on acetylene, not water, and that acetylene engines date to 1899 and have knock, pre-ignition and safety problems [4].
- **Likely real energy source:** **Calcium carbide.** It is made from lime and coke in electric-arc furnaces at very high temperature, an energy-intensive process, so water is only the co-reactant.

## 4. Govind Malviya — hydrogen motorcycle from water + aluminium (Bhopal, 2010)

- **Claim:** An electronics-diploma holder said his motorcycle starts on petrol and then switches to hydrogen. The hydrogen comes from water dripped onto **aluminium and sodium hydroxide**: 2 kg of the Al/NaOH mix with 2 litres of water gives about 200 g of H₂, enough for "about 100 km" [1].
- **Assessment:** No expert comment or test was reported [1]. The chemistry, 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂, is real, and ~200 g H₂ from ~2 kg Al is stoichiometrically plausible. However:
  - (a) 200 g of H₂ holds about 28 MJ (HHV), roughly the energy of 0.8 L of petrol, so 100 km on a motorcycle is optimistic.
  - (b) The reported liquefaction of hydrogen at "−106 °C" cannot be right, because hydrogen boils at about −253 °C. That detail was likely misreported.
- **Outcome:** Single Bhasha/AajTak report; no follow-up found.
- **Likely real energy source:** **Aluminium metal.** Its smelting takes far more electricity than the hydrogen returns.

**Sources**
1. [AajTak (Bhasha), "Hydrogen run motorcycle claim," Bhopal (19 Dec 2010)](https://www.aajtak.in/india/story/Hydrogen-run-motorcycle-claim-54306-2010-12-19)

# Part B — India: legitimate work that gets confused with "water engines"

## 11. Velozeta six-stroke engine (College of Engineering, Trivandrum, 2005–2007) — LEGITIMATE / DIFFERENT

- **What it is:** Four mechanical-engineering students at CET Trivandrum (U. Krishnaraj, Boby Sebastian, Arun Nair, Aaron Joseph George) developed a six-stroke engine. It **injects fresh air** during the exhaust stroke to get an extra expansion stroke and better scavenging. They claimed about 40% lower fuel consumption and a 65% CO reduction. It runs on petrol, diesel or LPG [1][2].
- **Why it is confused:** It is often lumped together with the US **Crower six-stroke** (2006), which injects **water** that flashes to steam using engine waste heat [1][3]. In both designs water or air is a working fluid recovering waste heat, **not a fuel**. The Crower patent application was later abandoned [1].
- **Energy source:** Conventional fuel. The extra stroke recovers otherwise-wasted heat.

## 13. VIT Chennai HHO dual-fuel engine study (2020) — LEGITIMATE RESEARCH

- **What it is:** Subramanian et al. (Vellore Institute of Technology, Chennai) studied a single-cylinder Kirloskar diesel engine at idle with hydrogen or HHO added at equal energy share. Notably, the HHO was **synthesised from bottled H₂ and O₂ in a 2:1 ratio**, so its energy is supplied and accounted for externally, not "free" from onboard electrolysis [1]. HHO reduced diesel use, CO₂ and unburnt HC. NOx and smoke results were mixed [1]. A related VIT paper reports brake thermal efficiency *decreasing* in nearly all hydrogen/HHO cases [2].
- **Why it matters:** This is how HHO is studied properly. Gas energy is counted as an input, so it shows emission effects, not a free-energy gain.

**Sources**
1. [Subramanian B. et al., "Emission Characteristic of a Dual fuel CI Engine Operating on Diesel + Hydrogen & Diesel + HHO gas…", IOP Conf. Ser.: Earth Environ. # Common patterns

1. **Hidden or unacknowledged conventional fuel.**
   - Ramar Pillai's "herbal petrol" was found to contain petroleum products, and a hollow stirrer filled with petrol was reported (entry 1).
   - Several "water kits" were demonstrated on cars whose petrol or diesel supply was never verifiably removed. Hoodbhoy's proposed test was exactly that: remove the fuel tank (entry 27).
2. **A consumable reactant is the real fuel; water is only a co-reactant.**
   - Calcium carbide → acetylene (Makrani, entry 3).
   - Aluminium + NaOH → hydrogen (Malviya, entry 4; Youngman, entry 28).
   - Metal hydride (likely Genepax, entry 25).
   - In each case the reactant was made with far more energy than it returns.
3. **Electrolysis loops powered by the battery or alternator (HHO / Brown's gas / "resonance").** These are always net-negative.
   - The Meyer court experts found "conventional electrolysis" (entry 20).
   - Controlled HHO tests found no fuel-economy gain (entry 30).
   - Stated currents (e.g., 0.5 A) are often orders of magnitude too small (entry 26).
4. **Secrecy and refusal of independent testing.**
   - Undisclosed "herbs," "catalysts," or "formulas" (entries 1, 2, 21, 25, 28, 29).
   - Meyer did not allow a scheduled independent examination (entry 20). Agha Waqar did not attend a NUST inspection (per op-ed, entry 27). Nikuba's inventor declined BRIN testing (entry 29).
5. **Investor, licence, or dealership solicitation, and the legal outcomes that followed.**
   - Meyer: dealerships, civil fraud finding (entry 20).
   - Dingel: Formosa funding, estafa conviction (entry 21).
   - Genesis World Energy: about 500 investors, theft plea (entry 22).
   - Ramar Pillai: public fuel sales, cheating conviction (entry 1).
6. **Official endorsement before verification.**
   - Tamil Nadu's government and DST officials (1996), Pakistan's cabinet (2012), and Nanyang's Party secretary (2019) all praised claims publicly before any independent test (entries 1, 27, 28).
   - Later retractions came slowly and quietly.
7. **"Foreign interest" and suppression narratives.**
   - Claimed Japanese launches (entry 2), Chinese or Dubai investors (entry 3), and Italian automakers (entry 29) are frequently cited and rarely documented.
   - Death or threat stories (Meyer, entry 20; Kumarasamy, entry 2) feed "suppressed invention" myths. In Meyer's case the coroner found natural causes.
8. **Media mislabelling of legitimate technology.**
   - Hydrogen fuel cells and hydrogen IC engines (entries 10, 14, 31), water injection and six-stroke cycles (entry 11), HHO combustion assist (entry 12), and solar water-splitting (entry 15) are routinely headlined as "runs on water."
   - In every legitimate case, the energy comes from hydrogen made elsewhere, from conventional fuel, or from sunlight.
9. **Fuel-price shocks drive revivals.** Waves of interest track fuel-price spikes and energy shortages: Pakistan's load-shedding in 2012 (entry 27), Indonesia in 2022–23 (entry 29), and the Philippines in 2026 (entry 25, source 3).
